BREWLY Growth Analytics

An end-to-end analytics demo for a device-and-refills membership brand where paid brand advertising is assumed unavailable: weekly KPIs from SQL marts, randomised CRM experiments with significance tests, a Bayesian marketing-mix model, a five-channel budget simulator, and an uplift model that decides who should get a CRM message.

Suggested path: Weekly dashboard → Experiments → Model check → Budget simulator → CRM targeting → Decision memo.

How to read this tab
  • Two experiments that measured CRM directly by splitting members at random, so the groups differ only in the messages they got.
  • Holdout: 30% of members got no messages for 8 weeks. The orders they lost are CRM's average effect.
  • Frequency test: 50% of members got about twice the messages for 6 weeks. Whether orders rose answers "do more messages pay?".
  • Sample-ratio check: do group sizes match the design (30:70, 50:50)? A p-value below 0.01 means the assignment broke and the result should not be trusted.
  • Table: difference in orders per member, the 95% confidence interval (the range likely to contain the true difference), the p-value (how likely a difference this size is by chance), and the minimum detectable effect (MDE). Green when the interval excludes zero and p < 0.05.
  • Chart: each region's difference and interval. A bar that crosses the zero line cannot be told apart from chance.

What the check found

CRM: attribution says 20, the truth is below zero at the margin. Crediting every order that follows a message makes CRM look like the best channel by far (about ₩20 of margin per won). The randomised holdout puts the average at ₩0.4-1.1, and doubling messages added nothing measurable. Running-low messages mostly reach members who were about to reorder anyway.

Why CRM is not in the marketing-mix model. Message volume rises with the number of members due to reorder, which also drives baseline orders. Six specifications that put CRM in the weekly model either contradicted the experiments or did not converge. CRM is therefore measured where it can be measured cleanly: member-level holdouts.

Acquisition: geo tests fix the retail-partner trap. Retail-partner incentives are paid per device sold, so they rise with demand. The observational model reads their next won at about ₩1.2 (truth ₩0.4); after the geo test, below break-even. Every acquisition break-even call now matches the truth (12 of 12, versus 9 of 12 before).

Still off: both models underrate brand events (₩0.4 vs ₩0.8-0.9); no test covers them. The CRM curve cannot dip below zero, so it reads the next message at about ₩0.1-0.25 instead of slightly negative; the call (cut) is the same.

What it means

The current journey ranks members by how likely they are to buy. Those are the members who need the message least: messaging the 70% most likely to buy keeps only about half of CRM's extra orders, worse than picking at random. Ranking by predicted uplift keeps about 86% with the same number of messages.

Who responds: members with fewer recent orders, a longer gap since their last order, and more often aged 20-39 (top decile: 1.6 orders in 12 weeks, 7.5 weeks since the last order) respond most. Heavy buyers (bottom decile: 3.2 orders, 3.5 weeks) buy anyway.

Caveats: the model is trained on one 8-week holdout, and predicted uplift is noisy for individuals (rank correlation with the truth 0.26). It is good at ordering groups, which is what targeting needs. It should be refreshed from a standing holdout each quarter.

Decision memo · 2026 growth plan

Send about 30% fewer CRM messages, chosen by uplift, and move the money to member referrals and store trials

To
BREWLY CRM and growth leads, Korea (fictional)
From
Digital Performance Analytics
Decision
Approve the channel mix and the CRM targeting rule below for 2026

Recommendation

Keep each region's total budget at its 2025 level. Cut CRM message volume by about 30% and stop messaging the members least likely to respond (rank by predicted uplift, not by likelihood to buy). Add about 30% to member referrals and 16-28% to store trials. Reduce retail-partner incentives by 7-18% and brand events by about 20%.

Why we believe the direction

  • CRM's next message does not pay back. The randomised holdout (30% of members, 8 weeks) cost 0.038 orders per member (95% CI 0.016-0.060, p < 0.001), an average return of ₩0.4-1.1 per won. Doubling messages for half the members added nothing measurable (p = 0.76). Attribution reports about ₩20 per won because messages go to members who were about to reorder.
  • Who gets the message matters as much as how many. Keeping 70% of messages chosen by predicted uplift keeps about 86% of CRM's extra orders; keeping the 70% most likely to buy keeps about 51%.
  • Referrals and store trials still pay back. The next won returns ₩1.0-1.5 in every region. Switching store trials off in Yeongnam for six weeks cost 84 (±15) new members, close to the model's 87.
  • Retail-partner incentives follow demand. They look strong in raw data; the Rest-of-Korea pause showed a small effect, and their next won returns below ₩1.

What could change the answer

  • Brand events: the model may underrate them, and no test covers them. Hold the cut at 20% until a test reads.
  • Yeongnam and Rest of Korea CRM: their own holdouts were too small to read alone (p = 0.36 and 0.20); the plan uses the national result.

Next steps

  1. Keep a standing 10% CRM holdout, and re-train the uplift model on it each quarter.
  2. Run a brand-events geo test in Q1 2026 (one region, 8 weeks).
  3. Cap messages per member per week, and refit the marketing-mix model each quarter before reallocating further.
Portfolio check against the hidden truth

Replayed on the true response, this plan captures 87% (Seoul metro), 78% (Yeongnam) and 88% (rest of Korea) of the best achievable gain. The plan an analyst would make before experiments (observational model plus attributed CRM) adds 30% to CRM and loses margin in every region (-11% to -31% of the best gain). The model expects less than the truth delivers (Seoul +₩38M vs +₩62M), so the ranges here are conservative.
